<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">0. The mistake people make about this platform</h2>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">When I describe it as "a budget modeller", people picture a spreadsheet with charts. That was true for about three weeks in December 2025.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">What it became is the system my business runs on. In one session a user can ask <em>what did we sell</em>, <em>who owns that account and are they on plan</em>, <em>can we build it</em>, <em>do we have the parts</em>, <em>did the supplier price move</em>, <em>will we be late and pay a penalty</em>, <em>what did engineering ship</em>, <em>what does the month-end pack say</em>. Every answer is drawn from the same data, the same engine and the same permission model.</p>
<blockquote style="margin:1.6em 0;padding:.7em 0 .7em 1.1em;border-left:4px solid #c2410c;color:#1f2933;font-size:1.06em;line-height:1.6;background:#fdf6f1">A finance dashboard reports the score. An operating system also reports why the score is<br>what it is, who is accountable for it, and what can still be changed.</blockquote>

<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">1. The constraint</h2>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">SAP ECC 6.0. No HANA, no Fiori, no OData. "Reporting" means running a transaction code, exporting an ALV grid, emailing the file.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><a href="https://www.tachyontech.com/sap-s-4hana-migration-costs-in-2026-what-mid-market-enterprises-should-actually-budget/" style="color:#c2410c;text-decoration:none;border-bottom:1px solid #f0c9b0">S/4HANA migrations start around $250K and run into millions</a>; Gartner expects ~17,000 of 35,000 ECC customers to still be on legacy past the <a href="https://www.cio.com/article/4000543/nearly-half-of-sap-ecc-customers-may-stick-with-legacy-erp-beyond-2027.html" style="color:#c2410c;text-decoration:none;border-bottom:1px solid #f0c9b0">2027 support deadline</a>. We're one of them.</p>
<blockquote style="margin:1.6em 0;padding:.7em 0 .7em 1.1em;border-left:4px solid #c2410c;color:#1f2933;font-size:1.06em;line-height:1.6;background:#fdf6f1">How do you get AI-native decision support out of an ERP with no API, without a<br>transformation budget, and without ever being wrong about a number?</blockquote>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The last clause is why this is mostly an architecture problem. An AI that is occasionally wrong about money is wrong fluently, at scale, and with convincing citations.</p>

<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">3. The working list</h2>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><a href="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v4-scc-05-customer-health.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener" style="display:block;border:0;text-decoration:none"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v4-scc-05-customer-health.png" alt="Customer Health &amp; Detailed Performance" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></a></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Status badges — <strong>Behind Plan · At Risk · Exceeded · Unplanned</strong> — beside Planned Margin vs Actual Margin, full-year budget, actual, three years of history, open order book and variance. Filterable by plan status and customer type. Copy / CSV / Excel.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Two design decisions separate this from an ordinary table:</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong><code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">UNPLANNED</code> is a first-class status.</strong> Revenue from a customer who was never in the plan is a fundamentally different conversation from a customer who beat their target. A single variance column would conflate the two, so the table reports them separately.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>"Planned Margin %" is explicitly labelled as the budget-model weighted margin — not an SAP gross margin.</strong> The column header says so. Two quantities that share a name will eventually be compared, and the comparison will be wrong. The correction belongs in the column header rather than a footnote.</p>

<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><a href="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v4-scc-explorer-by-am.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener" style="display:block;border:0;text-decoration:none"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v4-scc-explorer-by-am.png" alt="Variance by account manager" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></a><div style="font-size:.86em;color:#5b6673;text-align:center;margin:.5em auto 0;max-width:840px;line-height:1.5">Sales Performance Explorer: a pivot builder over the active scope. <p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Group by</strong> sales group · region · account manager · customer type · plan status · health status · customer. <strong>Split by</strong> customer type · plan status · health status · region. <strong>Metric</strong> variance · projected variance · actual YTD · YTD budget · open order book · customer count · pacing %. <strong>Sort</strong> worst / best variance, highest / lowest metric. <strong>Rows</strong> top 8/12/20/all.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Plus saved presets: <em>Regional Gap · Account Watchlist · Order Book Cover · Unplanned Revenue · Channel vs Municipal</em>.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">This is how a sales review is run: group by manager, sort by worst variance, and work down the list. The product carries the caption <em>"SAP actuals/order book stay separate from budget-model targets"</em> because the two are never silently merged. That separation is what gives the variance figure meaning.</p>

<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">7. Every widget can explain itself</h2>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><a href="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v4-scc-explainer.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener" style="display:block;border:0;text-decoration:none"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v4-scc-explainer.png" alt="Insight Explainer" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></a></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Every widget carries an <strong>Explain</strong> affordance that opens this drawer:</p>
<ul style="margin:1.1em 0 1.1em 1.3em;padding:0;line-height:1.65"><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>What this shows</strong> — in business language.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Data sources</strong> — with the exact ETL <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">defType</code> (<code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">sap-sales-insights</code>, <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">sap-orderbook-insights</code>, Budget Model).</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>How it is calculated</strong> — the actual formula and its assumptions.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>"Ask AI About This"</strong> — hands the widget's context straight to the copilot.</li></ul>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">This is the 170-entry semantic layer made visible. It answers <em>"where does this number come from"</em> without a Slack message, and it is the same catalog the AI reads through <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">get_insight_definition</code>. Because a single definition serves both the drawer and the copilot, the two explanations cannot drift apart.</p>

<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">9. Manufacturing production</h2>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><a href="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v3-production.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener" style="display:block;border:0;text-decoration:none"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v3-production.png" alt="Production Insights" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></a></div>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><a href="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v3-production-cap.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener" style="display:block;border:0;text-decoration:none"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v3-production-cap.png" alt="Capacity Planner" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></a><div style="font-size:.86em;color:#5b6673;text-align:center;margin:.5em auto 0;max-width:840px;line-height:1.5">Team roster → scenario levers (workday hours, working days, SA public holidays) → capacity, with quick scenarios and a pinned comparison strip. Explicitly read-only unless you are the Production Manager — and the page says so.</div></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Public holidays are modelled as <em>toggleable</em>: "work this day" is a real scenario, not a calendar assumption. And scenarios are pinned and compared, because the useful artefact of capacity planning is the delta between two plans.</p>
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">10. Stock — ship, build, unblock</h2>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><a href="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v3-stock.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener" style="display:block;border:0;text-decoration:none"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v3-stock.png" alt="Stock Management Insights" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></a></div>

Note the honesty: v1 answers are explicitly <strong><code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">gross_buildable</code></strong>, not MD04-reservation-aware free stock, and the UI says so. <div style="margin:1.8em 0"><a href="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/02-ai-engine-core.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener" style="display:block;border:0;text-decoration:none"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/02-ai-engine-core.png" alt="AI engine core" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></a></div>
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">14. Tools, not retrieval</h2>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">This is a database-query problem, not a document-search problem. Retrieval's characteristic failure is a confident answer drawn from approximately the right rows.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Routing has two layers, deliberately not interchangeable: the chat profile selects the model, and the tool profile selects which subset of the 83 tools is exposed. Both operate under hard budgets of 15 tool calls, 10 iterations, a 300-second orchestration ceiling and 60 seconds per provider call.</p>
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">15. The clarification gate</h2>
<div style="overflow-x:auto;margin:1.4em 0"><table style="border-collapse:collapse;width:100%;font-size:.94em;min-width:520px"><thead><tr><th style="text-align:left;padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:2px solid #e3e8ee;color:#c2410c;font-size:.9em;white-space:nowrap">Resolver outcome</th><th style="text-align:left;padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:2px solid #e3e8ee;color:#c2410c;font-size:.9em;white-space:nowrap">Action</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top">not resolved</td><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top"><strong>clarify</strong> — stop and ask</td></tr><tr><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top">confidence &lt; 0.70</td><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top"><strong>clarify</strong> — catches all-token guesses</td></tr><tr><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top">0.70 – 0.90</td><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top"><strong>echo</strong> — "reading X as Y — correct me"</td></tr><tr><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top">≥ 0.90</td><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top"><strong>answer</strong> — proceed silently</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Where the reference is ambiguous, the handler returns candidates and no data. The comment explaining that decision generalises well beyond this codebase:</p>
<blockquote style="margin:1.6em 0;padding:.7em 0 .7em 1.1em;border-left:4px solid #c2410c;color:#1f2933;font-size:1.06em;line-height:1.6;background:#fdf6f1"><em>"The system prompt already instructs 'if ambiguous, ASK' and the model still guessed-and-proceeded. <strong>You cannot make the model ask by instructing it harder; you make answering impossible without asking by withholding the data.</strong>"</em></blockquote>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">To obtain a behaviour from a model, change what is possible rather than what is instructed.</p>
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">16. Model auto-discovery and the health breaker</h2>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><a href="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/08-model-discovery-and-routing.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener" style="display:block;border:0;text-decoration:none"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/08-model-discovery-and-routing.png" alt="Model discovery and routing" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></a></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The platform is not pinned to a model string in an environment variable. It calls Anthropic's <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">/v1/models</code> on boot and every 12 hours, classifies by tier (<code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">fable → opus → sonnet → haiku</code>), flags beta builds behind an admin opt-in, regex-validates ids, and persists a catalog to Cosmos so the admin dropdown renders without a browser ever calling Anthropic.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Failure posture is safe defaults, never throw: empty catalog falls back to the manual priority list; a failed refresh retains the stale catalog and surfaces the error to admins only.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Auto mode walks the tier order and manual mode follows an admin list. A demoted model moves to the back of the order rather than out of it, so the result is degradation rather than an outage. One resolver binds every Claude call: chat, Composer dry-runs and scheduled agents alike.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The health breaker takes observations from <em>chat</em> (which hits the provider orders of magnitude more than a weekly agent) and thresholds from admin policy. Agents consume its verdict but never write to it — one run is a sample size of one.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The source is candid about the limitation. A model matching no tier pattern lands in <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">other</code>, which auto mode never selects: <em>"this is exactly how claude-fable-5 was missed."</em></p>
<blockquote style="margin:1.6em 0;padding:.7em 0 .7em 1.1em;border-left:4px solid #c2410c;color:#1f2933;font-size:1.06em;line-height:1.6;background:#fdf6f1">Model choice is operational configuration rather than a deployment. When a new frontier<br>model ships, an admin selects it from a dropdown that populated itself. When one degrades,<br>the platform routes around it and recovers. Neither event requires an engineer.</blockquote>
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">17. Designed around the Claude Agent SDK</h2>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><a href="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/09-claude-sdk-anatomy.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener" style="display:block;border:0;text-decoration:none"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/09-claude-sdk-anatomy.png" alt="Claude SDK anatomy" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></a></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong><code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">runtime.js</code> is the only file importing the SDK.</strong> SDK types may not appear in stores, hooks, the tool server or <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">ai-core</code>; the SDK's <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">tool()</code> and <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">createSdkMcpServer()</code> are <em>injected</em> into the tool server. The cost of replacing the SDK is roughly a week spent rewiring that one file. Tests inject a fake async iterator, so the entire agent runtime is exercisable with no network and no SDK present.</p>
<pre style="background:#0f1720;color:#e6edf3;padding:1em 1.1em;border-radius:8px;overflow-x:auto;font-size:.86em;line-height:1.55;margin:1.4em 0;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace"><code>const options = {
  systemPrompt: composeAgentRunSystemPrompt({ agentSystemPrompt: def.systemPrompt }),
  model: selectedModel,                        // admin policy → def.model → SDK default
  disallowedTools: buildDisallowedToolsList(), // all 19 SDK built-ins
  allowedTools: allowedMcpNames,               // mcp__syntell__{logical}
  mcpServers: { syntell: toolServerInstance },
  permissionMode: 'dontAsk',
  maxTurns: def.quotas?.maxTurns ?? 16,
  abortController: sdkAbortController,
  hooks: {
    PreToolUse:  [{ matcher: '.*', hooks: [policyGuard] }],
    PostToolUse: [{ matcher: '.*', hooks: [citation, dataset, audit] }],
    Stop:        [{ matcher: '.*', hooks: [cost] }]
  }
};</code></pre>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Four SDK behaviours each cost me a day or more: <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">tools: []</code> silently disables <em>all</em> tools including MCP ones — omit the key entirely; <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">alwaysLoad: true</code> is required or MCP tools hide behind built-in tool search; <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">tool_response</code> is a JSON <em>string</em> rather than an object; <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">console.log</code> from a hook never reaches container stdout.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>One truth layer, two surfaces.</strong> The agent prompt is composed from the same exported blocks as the chat prompt, assembled at runtime and never persisted. Improving the grounding therefore requires no document migration, and drift becomes structurally impossible rather than a matter of discipline.</p>
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">18. Two behaviours, and the bug they revealed</h2>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><a href="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/ai09-honest-failure-and-inference-split.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener" style="display:block;border:0;text-decoration:none"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/ai09-honest-failure-and-inference-split.png" alt="Honest failure" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></a></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>It refuses:</strong> <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">SOURCE: none — tools unavailable</code>, naming all three timed-out Jira queries. <strong>It separates fact from inference:</strong> <em>"Facts above are tool-derived; the … points are my inferences/recommendations."</em></p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">I re-ran that question for this edition and it timed out again, on three queries out of three. The degradation was better this time: it fell back to the Roadmap page KPIs, which carry the same live Jira data already resident in Cosmos, produced a quantified answer on throughput and work in progress, and refused only the parts it could not source.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">So I read the code. <strong>Root cause:</strong> <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">TOOL_TIMEOUT_MS = 5000</code> is the default. Production tools were recognised as slow and given a 30s override. No Jira tool was ever added to that map — so a cross-internet Atlassian call gets the same five seconds as an in-region Cosmos point read. Full write-up.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The fix is not a larger constant. Derive the timeout from a declared <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">executionClass</code>, then add a contract test asserting that every outbound non-Azure tool declares <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">external-api</code>. A silent default then becomes a build failure.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">The pattern worth naming: beware the opt-in registry</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Two of this platform's real defects share a shape: the missed model tier and the Jira timeout. Both were maps that new items had to be added to, where forgetting to add one failed silently. The remedy in each case is to derive the entry from a declared property, then test the derivation.</p>
<hr style="border:0;border-top:1px solid #e3e8ee;margin:2.4em 0">
<h2 style="font-size:1.5em;line-height:1.25;margin:2.6em 0 .2em;color:#c2410c;letter-spacing:-.01em;border-top:3px solid #c2410c;padding-top:.7em">Part V — The agent platform</h2>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><a href="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/03-agent-framework.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener" style="display:block;border:0;text-decoration:none"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/03-agent-framework.png" alt="Agent framework" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></a></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Five decisions: quarantine the SDK · lock the built-in tool surface · <strong>enforce policy in the handler, because the SDK can block a tool <em>call</em> but cannot filter its <em>output</strong></em> · extract citations structurally from tool returns · validate before publishable, with failures to a Needs Review queue.</p>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><a href="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/04-composer-v2.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener" style="display:block;border:0;text-decoration:none"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/04-composer-v2.png" alt="Composer V2" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></a></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Chat-first authoring without a second chat runtime. <strong>The LLM has no publish tool.</strong> The invariant that made it shippable: <strong>V1-indistinguishability</strong>, enforced by a snapshot suite.</p>

<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">20. Lessons</h2>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Build the calculation engine first</strong>, as a pure function.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Give agents tools, not documents.</strong></p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Enforce policy where the data is, not where the prompt is.</strong></p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>You cannot make a model behave by instructing it harder — change what is possible.</strong></p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Keep the deterministic layer deterministic.</strong></p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Sequence the boring dependency first.</strong> Identity before Movement Pack; Movement Pack before agents.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Prove conservation before you publish.</strong></p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>A silent no-op is worse than a crash.</strong> The scheduler's <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">findDue</code> filtered on <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">c.enabled = true</code> — a field <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">AgentDefinitionV1</code> does not have. The predicate never matched, so no scheduled agent run could ever fire, and nothing errored.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Beware the opt-in registry.</strong></p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Write contracts, not prompts.</strong> A prompt expresses an intention; a contract is checkable, and only one of the two survives a refactor.</p>

<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"Why didn't you just use LangChain?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The primary reason is that I set out to learn this from the ground up. I wanted to understand what a tool loop, a context window, a routing decision and a hallucination are, rather than learn a framework's abstraction over them. A framework would have defeated that goal.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">There is also an architectural answer, and it stands on its own. The table below sets the suite against what this platform actually required:</p>

<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The decisive point is that the agent loop was never the hard part. Anthropic's Agent SDK supplied the tool loop, sub-agents, hooks, permissions and session handling. The research note that locked in the decision records that the SDK "saves us from rebuilding the runtime / orchestration layer," reducing v0 from weeks of work to days.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The hard part was everything a framework cannot supply: ABAC enforced inside the tool handlers before data reaches the model; the Movement Pack, which pre-computes typed, ranked, content-addressed deltas so that the model narrates a result rather than computing one; a 170-entry governed insight catalog; and an SAP reconciliation gate that refuses to publish when row counts drift. LangChain has no opinion about any of it, because these are invariants specific to my business. Adopting a framework would have placed an abstraction over the tractable part of the problem and left the difficult part untouched.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Cost is a real constraint here rather than a rhetorical one. The platform runs on a small budget for roughly twelve internal users. LangSmith's Plus tier is $39 per seat per month plus trace overages, and a typical production pairing of LangSmith with LangGraph Platform lands between $175 and $375 a month before model spend. For a one-person team that is a material line item, and each dependency is also something that can break, change licence or reprice.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Where I would change my mind.</strong> My two weakest dimensions are evaluation and observability, both scored 2 out of 5, and both are LangSmith's core competence. The sensible response is not a framework rewrite but the adoption of LangSmith, promptfoo, Braintrust or DeepEval for the evaluation and tracing layer specifically, against a codebase whose interfaces are already frozen and contract-tested. The aim would be to close a named gap rather than to adopt a framework wholesale.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"Why go all-in on Claude? Why not build a provider-agnostic router?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">I considered it, and the architecture still carries the seam. <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">AiProviderTransport</code> is a frozen, asserted contract — the orchestration layer talks to <em>a provider</em>, not to Anthropic. <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">providerCapabilities.js</code> says so in its own header: adding OpenAI or Gemini "would add sibling files (<code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">openai.js</code>, <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">gemini.js</code>) with the same <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">classify()</code> / <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">resolveThinkingConfig()</code> contract."</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Precision matters here, because this is the point at which such claims are usually inflated. There is a seam, not a router: one implementation exists. The maturity audit scores the dimension 3 out of 5 and names the gap directly as "sophisticated single-provider routing; no cross-provider failover."</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Stopping there was a resourcing decision rather than an architectural belief. When you are building at speed you choose a platform and proceed. I am one person running an entire business, and a provider-abstraction layer is a permanent tax on every feature, paid daily against a risk that has not yet materialised. I therefore integrated vertically on Anthropic: not only the LLM API but the Agent SDK, the MCP tool surface and the hook model. That verticality is what reduced the agent framework from months of work to days.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Inside that bet the platform does carry automatic failover: model auto-discovery every twelve hours against <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">/v1/models</code>, tier classification, and a health circuit breaker that demotes and recovers without intervention. The failover operates within the model family rather than across vendors. In a total Anthropic outage the AI features degrade while the rest of the platform does not, because every number on every screen originates in Cosmos and the model only narrates it.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">I'd revisit on a hard trigger: a sustained availability problem, a pricing shift, or a capability that only exists elsewhere. The seam is there so that day is an implementation, not a rewrite.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"Why Cosmos DB and not a relational database?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The choice began as a cost decision and survived for engineering reasons. What started as a proof of concept on a small budget grew into an enterprise application, and a document store, structured with discipline, turned out to fit the problem better than the relational default:</p>
<ul style="margin:1.1em 0 1.1em 1.3em;padding:0;line-height:1.65"><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Schema evolution without migrations.</strong> Fiscal-year structures, contract shapes and connector outputs changed repeatedly over eight months. Not one of those changes needed a migration window.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>The ETL has no impedance mismatch.</strong> SAP exports arrive as records and land as documents. A connector run produces artefacts and synchronises documents, with no ORM, no staging schema and no mapping layer between the export and the store. That accounts for much of why the ETL is ten connectors rather than a data-engineering department.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>The partition key <em>is</em> the security boundary.</strong> Everything partitions on <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">/pk</code>, carrying business unit and fiscal year. The authorization rule is <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">hasPermission AND hasBuScope AND ownsResource</code>, and business-unit scope is enforced in the physical storage layout rather than in a <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">WHERE</code> clause that someone can forget to write.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Operational features I'd otherwise have built.</strong> TTL gives one-year audit retention as a field, not a cron job. ETags give optimistic concurrency without a locking strategy.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>One dependency, one region, an emulator for local dev.</strong> <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">npm run dev:up</code> boots the emulator; there is no second database technology to run, secure, back up or patch.</li></ul>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The counterpoint is real: cross-container joins and aggregations are my problem rather than the engine's, and they are hand-written in the application layer. A relational database would make ad-hoc analytical queries considerably easier. That is also why the AI reads a governed semantic layer of roughly 170 curated insights instead of composing SQL. The storage decision and the tool-catalog decision follow from the same premise.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"Why a fixed tool catalog instead of text-to-SQL?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Because a generated query cannot be authorized in advance. Each of the 83 tools enforces ABAC inside the handler, before a single row reaches the model, and returns the citations that the hooks accumulate. Text-to-SQL inverts that arrangement: the model composes the access path and the audit happens afterwards. Nor can a generated query be governed. dbt's 2026 benchmark makes the case for a semantic layer in preference to text-to-SQL, and the insight catalog is that layer, versioned with its formulas, assumptions and sources.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"How do you stop it inventing numbers?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The mechanism is structural rather than instructional. Deterministic tool handlers emit the citations and chart datasets, <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">PostToolUse</code> hooks accumulate them, and the model is never the source of provenance. The Movement Pack pre-computes typed, ranked, ABAC-scoped deltas with content-addressed identifiers, so the model narrates a computed fact rather than performing arithmetic. Below a confidence threshold the clarification gate withholds the data outright instead of instructing the model to take more care.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">One part of that apparatus does not yet work. The <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">ClaimV1</code>/<code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">FactV1</code> refine gate passes trivially, because a boilerplate fact is always attached. The audit caught it, it is documented, and it sits on the near-term list. A guarantee that always passes is not a guarantee.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"What's the weakest part of the platform?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Durable execution. The dispatch queue is written but has no consumer, so a process restart orphans any in-flight run. The scheduler's stale-lease window of five minutes is shorter than the fifteen-minute run timeout, which leaves a latent double-fire. This is a documented Phase 4 deferral rather than an oversight, and the client mitigates it with auto-resume, but it remains the single largest reliability gap and the one place where LangGraph's checkpointing would have bought me something real.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"No evals? In a financial application?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Correct, and it heads the backlog. What exists is the opposite trade: more than 1,100 deterministic contract tests, frozen versioned schemas, and a named regression test for every incident, including penalty-netting, the order-book double-count and the M20 cabinet. Every model call in every test is a deterministic fake. That yields strong guarantees about behaviour and none about answer quality. The probabilistic layer belongs on top of the deterministic gate rather than in place of it.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"How does one person keep this from rotting?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">A written contract carrying a pre-review self-check, each item of which exists because of a specific and embarrassing miss: assert the observable result rather than the intermediate you produced; diff both sides of every interface; run the full gate after touching a contract-tested artefact; enumerate every entry point; declare no silent caps; accept no prompt-only correctness. The recurring root cause it was written to eliminate is stopping at "my code does what I intended" instead of "the consumer gets the right result." Appendix A records that same lesson failing eight further times in a single afternoon.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"What would you do differently?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Instrument with OpenTelemetry from the first day, because retrofitting <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">gen_ai.*</code> spans over a bespoke correlation scheme is pure rework. Build the evaluation harness before the twentieth feature rather than after it. And enforce a control before declaring it: the sharpest finding in the audit was a class of controls that were persisted yet inert, where an admin sets <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">restrictedFields</code> in the interface and believes it takes effect. Documentation describing runtime behaviour you have not written is worse than no documentation, for the same reason that a test agreeing with your bug is worse than no test.</p>
<hr style="border:0;border-top:1px solid #e3e8ee;margin:2.4em 0">
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em" id="appendix-a">Appendix A — how these screenshots were made</h2>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Every image is the live production system with real data. A redaction layer is injected before capture; capture loops, redact, verify, repeat, and writes a PNG only once the page verifies clean.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">It failed eight times, and each failure was a variation on one mistake: the checker only ever saw what the redactor saw.</p>
<ol style="margin:1.1em 0 1.1em 1.3em;padding:0;line-height:1.65"><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Both scanned leaf elements.</strong> Prose inside <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">&lt;li&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Facts:&lt;/strong&gt;…&lt;/li&gt;</code> is not a leaf; real amounts reached a screenshot while the check said "clean".</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Pattern matching was the wrong model.</strong> An answer named five customers in prose with no currency, no suffix, no digits. <strong>You cannot enumerate what sensitive text looks like</strong> — so AI output became sensitive <em>by default</em>.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>I reintroduced bug 1 in the fix for bug 2.</strong></li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Form values are rendered text that is not a text node.</strong> <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">&lt;input value="Elihle"&gt;</code> shows an employee's name; a <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">TreeWalker</code> never sees it. The Capacity Planner roster published four real first names.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Names that only exist in the page's own data.</strong> The Sales Command Center renders the active account manager as a scope chip. No hardcoded regex can know that "ZIYAAD" is a person — but the page's own Account Manager filter can. The fix <strong>harvests names from the people-picker options and redacts them everywhere</strong>, which generalises to any business unit without editing the code.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Tenant identifiers that look like ordinary codes.</strong> A business-unit slug and a Jira project key are indistinguishable from a product code to a regex. Hardcoding them in the redactor would have repeated this repo's own <strong>opt-in-registry</strong> bug — the pattern behind both the missed model tier and the Jira timeout, where forgetting an entry fails <em>silently</em>. They live in one JSON file that feeds the redactor <strong>and</strong> the checker, so adding a term extends both at once.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>The length threshold was the wrong test for tables.</strong> AI output is blurred by default above 25 characters — but the model also answers with <strong>markdown tables</strong>, and a Jira issue title is 17 characters. Cells slipped under the bar and published. Length never described the risk; <em>being tabular data</em> did. Cells are now blurred unconditionally inside an AI surface.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Verified clean, captured dirty.</strong> The worst one. The loop proved the DOM was clean <em>before</em> the screenshot — the intermediate, not the deliverable. Taking a clipped capture resizes the rendering surface, Recharts re-renders its axes, and brand-new <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">&lt;tspan&gt;</code>s carrying real currency (<code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">R 400 000</code>, <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">R 1 600 000</code>) were painted into the PNG <strong>after</strong> the check passed. The run logged <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">$=0</code> and the file leaked. The assertion now <strong>straddles</strong> the capture: shoot, re-verify, re-shoot if the page moved — so the number reported describes the file on disk.</li></ol>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Mechanical gotchas: <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">captureBeyondViewport</code> renders offscreen content <em>at capture time</em>, i.e. after redaction; CSS <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">filter</code> does nothing on an SVG <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">&lt;tspan&gt;</code>; <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">element.click()</code> does not close a React dropdown, because outside-click handlers listen for <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;color:#1f2933;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">mousedown</code> — which left the fiscal-year panel open across <em>every</em> screenshot of one entire run; and the physical mouse cursor sits wherever it was left, so a chart re-rendering underneath it draws a hover tooltip straight into the capture.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">There is a pleasing symmetry with the SEV1 in §20 and the Jira bug. <strong>A test that agrees with your bug is worse than no test</strong> — it converts an unknown risk into false confidence. The fix, every time, was to assert the <em>observable outcome</em> rather than re-implement the producer's logic.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">One screenshot is deliberately published readable — the "it refuses" answer in §18. Every visible string was reviewed by hand.</p>
