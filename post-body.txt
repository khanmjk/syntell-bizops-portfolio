<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">0. The mistake people make about this platform</h2>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">When I describe it as "a budget modeller", people picture a spreadsheet with charts. That was true for about three weeks in December 2025.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">What it became is the system my business runs on. In one session a user can ask <em>what did we sell</em>, <em>who owns that account and are they on plan</em>, <em>can we build it</em>, <em>do we have the parts</em>, <em>did the supplier price move</em>, <em>will we be late and pay a penalty</em>, <em>what did engineering ship</em>, <em>what does the month-end pack say</em> — and get answers from the same data, the same engine, the same permission model.</p>
<blockquote style="margin:1.6em 0;padding:.7em 0 .7em 1.1em;border-left:4px solid #c2410c;color:#1f2933;font-size:1.06em;line-height:1.6;background:#fdf6f1">A finance dashboard tells you the score.<br>An operating system tells you <strong>why</strong> the score is what it is, <strong>who owns it</strong>, and what you can still change.</blockquote>

<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">1. The constraint</h2>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">SAP ECC 6.0. No HANA, no Fiori, no OData. "Reporting" means running a transaction code, exporting an ALV grid, emailing the file.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><a href="https://www.tachyontech.com/sap-s-4hana-migration-costs-in-2026-what-mid-market-enterprises-should-actually-budget/" style="color:#c2410c;text-decoration:none;border-bottom:1px solid #f0c9b0">S/4HANA migrations start around $250K and run into millions</a>; Gartner expects ~17,000 of 35,000 ECC customers to still be on legacy past the <a href="https://www.cio.com/article/4000543/nearly-half-of-sap-ecc-customers-may-stick-with-legacy-erp-beyond-2027.html" style="color:#c2410c;text-decoration:none;border-bottom:1px solid #f0c9b0">2027 support deadline</a>. We're one of them.</p>
<blockquote style="margin:1.6em 0;padding:.7em 0 .7em 1.1em;border-left:4px solid #c2410c;color:#1f2933;font-size:1.06em;line-height:1.6;background:#fdf6f1"><strong>How do you get AI-native decision support out of an ERP that cannot give you an API — without a transformation budget, and without ever being wrong about a number?</strong></blockquote>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">That last clause is why this is mostly about architecture. An AI that is occasionally wrong about money is wrong <em>fluently</em>, at scale, with convincing citations.</p>

<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The organising idea is a single filter bar that re-scopes the entire page:</p>
<pre style="background:#0f1720;color:#e6edf3;padding:1em 1.1em;border-radius:8px;overflow-x:auto;font-size:.86em;line-height:1.55;margin:1.4em 0;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace"><code>ACCOUNT MANAGER  ·  REGION GROUP  ·  REGION  ·  SPECIFIC CUSTOMER   [Reset]
      │
      └─ every KPI, chart, table and drill-down below re-computes
         against the selected scope</code></pre>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Four hero KPIs — <strong>Total Portfolio Budget · Actual (Full Year) · Full-Year Variance · Open Order Book</strong> — then seven analytical sections, all scope-aware.</p>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v4-scc-all-hero.png" alt="Sales Command Center — full portfolio" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"><div style="font-size:.86em;color:#5b6673;text-align:center;margin:.5em auto 0;max-width:840px;line-height:1.5">Full portfolio, with Gap to Budget Analysis: actual vs latest forecast vs budget target, plus Final Attainment and Forecast Visibility bars.</div></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The organisational consequence is bigger than the feature. The GM and the account manager look at <strong>the same page</strong>, not two reports that disagree. A portfolio review stops being "send me your numbers" and becomes "let's both look at the same scope".</p>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v4-scc-am-hero.png" alt="Scoped to one account manager" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"><div style="font-size:.86em;color:#5b6673;text-align:center;margin:.5em auto 0;max-width:840px;line-height:1.5">The same page, scoped to one account manager. Note the scope chip on every widget, and "Drill down through your portfolio" with BACK navigation.</div></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Nothing was rebuilt for the account-manager view. It is the same components, re-scoped — which is precisely why a manager's numbers cannot diverge from management's.</p>
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">3. The working list</h2>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v4-scc-05-customer-health.png" alt="Customer Health &amp; Detailed Performance" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Status badges — <strong>Behind Plan · At Risk · Exceeded · Unplanned</strong> — beside Planned Margin vs Actual Margin, full-year budget, actual, three years of history, open order book and variance. Filterable by plan status and customer type. Copy / CSV / Excel.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Two design decisions are worth pulling out, because they are the difference between a table and a management instrument:</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong><code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">UNPLANNED</code> is a first-class status.</strong> Revenue from a customer who was never in the plan is a fundamentally different conversation from a customer who beat their target. A single "variance" column would blur them; the table refuses to.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>"Planned Margin %" is explicitly labelled as the budget-model weighted margin — not an SAP gross margin.</strong> The column header says so. Two things called <em>margin</em> that mean different things is how trust in a finance tool dies, and the fix is a label, not a footnote.</p>
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">4. The same portfolio, four ways</h2>

<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>FULL YEAR / QUARTERLY / 12 MONTHS</strong> changes the attainment question. <strong>Net Sales / Gross Profit / Quantity</strong> changes what "performance" means — a rep can hit revenue and miss margin, and in a hardware business revenue can hold while unit volume falls because price moved. Only the quantity lens shows you that.</p>

<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">5. The Explorer — self-service without a BI licence</h2>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v4-scc-explorer-by-am.png" alt="Variance by account manager" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"><div style="font-size:.86em;color:#5b6673;text-align:center;margin:.5em auto 0;max-width:840px;line-height:1.5">Sales Performance Explorer: a pivot builder over the active scope. Here — variance by account manager, split by customer type, with a linked account-detail panel.</div></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Group by</strong> sales group · region · <strong>account manager</strong> · customer type · plan status · health status · customer. <strong>Split by</strong> customer type · plan status · health status · region. <strong>Metric</strong> variance · projected variance · actual YTD · YTD budget · open order book · customer count · pacing %. <strong>Sort</strong> worst / best variance, highest / lowest metric. <strong>Rows</strong> top 8/12/20/all.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Plus saved presets: <em>Regional Gap · Account Watchlist · Order Book Cover · Unplanned Revenue · Channel vs Municipal</em>.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">This is the answer to "how do you run a sales review": group by manager, sort by worst variance, and the conversation starts itself. Note the caption the product carries — <em>"SAP actuals/order book stay separate from budget-model targets"</em>. The two worlds are never silently merged, which is the whole reason the variance number means anything.</p>
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">6. Three levels, one surface</h2>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v4-scc-orderbook.png" alt="Customer order book inspection" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Portfolio → customer → <strong>individual open order lines</strong>, without leaving the page or changing tool. One permission model the whole way down: an account manager sees their own accounts' lines, and the ABAC check happens server-side regardless of what the UI shows.</p>
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">7. Every widget can explain itself</h2>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v4-scc-explainer.png" alt="Insight Explainer" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Every widget carries an <strong>Explain</strong> affordance that opens this drawer:</p>
<ul style="margin:1.1em 0 1.1em 1.3em;padding:0;line-height:1.65"><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>What this shows</strong> — in business language.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Data sources</strong> — with the exact ETL <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">defType</code> (<code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">sap-sales-insights</code>, <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">sap-orderbook-insights</code>, Budget Model).</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>How it is calculated</strong> — the actual formula and its assumptions.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>"Ask AI About This"</strong> — hands the widget's context straight to the copilot.</li></ul>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">This is the 170-entry semantic layer made visible. It answers <em>"where does this number come from"</em> without a Slack message — and it is <strong>the same catalog the AI reads</strong> via <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">get_insight_definition</code>. One definition, two consumers: the human and the model. That is why the copilot's explanation of a metric and the drawer's explanation cannot drift apart.</p>

<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">11. Procurement</h2>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v3-supplier-po.png" alt="Supplier PO Insights" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"><div style="font-size:.86em;color:#5b6673;text-align:center;margin:.5em auto 0;max-width:840px;line-height:1.5">Seven tabs over purchase orders, PO lines and GRN receipts, with a procurement scope filter and a <strong>data-quality</strong> hero card.</div></div>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v3-po-runway.png" alt="Inbound runway" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></div>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/v3-po-trends.png" alt="Supplier trends" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The scope filter is a semantic decision made in the ETL, not a UI convenience: product materials, product-adjacent, business overhead and unclassified spend are different signals, and mixing them makes every ratio meaningless. And promoting <em>data quality</em> to a hero KPI is the opposite of what most dashboards do — the honest answer to "how much are we committed to" depends on how much of the PO book could be classified at all.</p>

<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">14. Tools, not retrieval</h2>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>This is not a document-search problem; it's a database-query problem.</strong> Retrieval's failure mode is a confident answer from <em>approximately</em> the right rows.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Two layers of routing, explicitly <strong>not interchangeable</strong> — chat profile = <em>which brain</em>, tool profile = <em>which subset of the 83 tools</em>. Hard budgets: 15 tool calls, 10 iterations, 300s ceiling, 60s per provider call.</p>
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">15. The clarification gate</h2>
<div style="overflow-x:auto;margin:1.4em 0"><table style="border-collapse:collapse;width:100%;font-size:.94em;min-width:520px"><thead><tr><th style="text-align:left;padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:2px solid #e3e8ee;color:#c2410c;font-size:.9em;white-space:nowrap">Resolver outcome</th><th style="text-align:left;padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:2px solid #e3e8ee;color:#c2410c;font-size:.9em;white-space:nowrap">Action</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top">not resolved</td><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top"><strong>clarify</strong> — stop and ask</td></tr><tr><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top">confidence &lt; 0.70</td><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top"><strong>clarify</strong> — catches all-token guesses</td></tr><tr><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top">0.70 – 0.90</td><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top"><strong>echo</strong> — "reading X as Y — correct me"</td></tr><tr><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top">≥ 0.90</td><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top"><strong>answer</strong> — proceed silently</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">When ambiguous, the handler returns <strong>candidates only, no data</strong>. The comment explaining why is the most portable sentence in the codebase:</p>
<blockquote style="margin:1.6em 0;padding:.7em 0 .7em 1.1em;border-left:4px solid #c2410c;color:#1f2933;font-size:1.06em;line-height:1.6;background:#fdf6f1"><em>"The system prompt already instructs 'if ambiguous, ASK' and the model still guessed-and-proceeded. <strong>You cannot make the model ask by instructing it harder; you make answering impossible without asking by withholding the data.</strong>"</em></blockquote>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">If you want a behaviour from a model, don't ask harder — change what is possible.</p>
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">16. Model auto-discovery and the health breaker</h2>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/08-model-discovery-and-routing.png" alt="Model discovery and routing" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The platform is <strong>not pinned to a model string in an env var</strong>. It calls Anthropic's <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">/v1/models</code> on boot and every 12 hours, classifies by tier (<code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">fable → opus → sonnet → haiku</code>), flags beta builds behind an admin opt-in, regex-validates ids, and persists a catalog to Cosmos so the admin dropdown renders without a browser ever calling Anthropic.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Failure posture is safe defaults, never throw: empty catalog falls back to the manual priority list; a failed refresh retains the stale catalog and surfaces the error to admins only.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Auto mode walks the tier order; manual follows an admin list; <strong>demoted models go to the back, not away</strong> — degradation, not outage. The <strong>same resolver binds every Claude call</strong>: chat, Composer dry-runs, scheduled agents.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The health breaker takes observations from <em>chat</em> (which hits the provider orders of magnitude more than a weekly agent) and thresholds from admin policy. Agents <strong>consume</strong> its verdict but never <strong>write</strong> to it — one run is a sample size of one.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">And the honest half, in the source: a model matching no tier pattern lands in <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">other</code>, which auto mode never selects — <em>"this is exactly how claude-fable-5 was missed."</em></p>
<blockquote style="margin:1.6em 0;padding:.7em 0 .7em 1.1em;border-left:4px solid #c2410c;color:#1f2933;font-size:1.06em;line-height:1.6;background:#fdf6f1"><strong>Model choice is operational configuration, not a deployment.</strong> A new frontier model ships → an admin picks it from a dropdown that populated itself. One degrades → the platform routes around it and recovers. Neither needs an engineer.</blockquote>
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">17. Designed around the Claude Agent SDK</h2>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/09-claude-sdk-anatomy.png" alt="Claude SDK anatomy" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong><code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">runtime.js</code> is the only file importing the SDK.</strong> SDK types may not appear in stores, hooks, the tool server or <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">ai-core</code>; the SDK's <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">tool()</code> and <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">createSdkMcpServer()</code> are <em>injected</em> into the tool server. Swap cost: about a week rewiring one file. Tests inject a fake async iterator — the whole agent runtime is testable with no network and no SDK.</p>
<pre style="background:#0f1720;color:#e6edf3;padding:1em 1.1em;border-radius:8px;overflow-x:auto;font-size:.86em;line-height:1.55;margin:1.4em 0;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace"><code>const options = {
  systemPrompt: composeAgentRunSystemPrompt({ agentSystemPrompt: def.systemPrompt }),
  model: selectedModel,                        // admin policy → def.model → SDK default
  disallowedTools: buildDisallowedToolsList(), // all 19 SDK built-ins
  allowedTools: allowedMcpNames,               // mcp__syntell__{logical}
  mcpServers: { syntell: toolServerInstance },
  permissionMode: 'dontAsk',
  maxTurns: def.quotas?.maxTurns ?? 16,
  abortController: sdkAbortController,
  hooks: {
    PreToolUse:  [{ matcher: '.*', hooks: [policyGuard] }],
    PostToolUse: [{ matcher: '.*', hooks: [citation, dataset, audit] }],
    Stop:        [{ matcher: '.*', hooks: [cost] }]
  }
};</code></pre>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Four gotchas that cost real days:</strong> <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">tools: []</code> silently disables <em>all</em> tools including MCP ones — omit the key entirely; <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">alwaysLoad: true</code> is required or MCP tools hide behind built-in tool search; <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">tool_response</code> is a JSON <strong>string</strong>, not an object; <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">console.log</code> from a hook never reaches container stdout.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>One truth layer, two surfaces.</strong> The agent prompt is composed from the <em>same exported blocks</em> as the chat prompt, runtime-composed and never persisted — so improving grounding needs no document migration, and <strong>drift is structurally impossible rather than a discipline problem</strong>.</p>
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">18. Two behaviours, and the bug they revealed</h2>
<div style="margin:1.8em 0"><img src="https://khanmjk.github.io/syntell-bizops-portfolio/img/ai09-honest-failure-and-inference-split.png" alt="Honest failure" style="display:block;width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:8px"></div>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>It refuses:</strong> <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">SOURCE: none — tools unavailable</code>, naming all three timed-out Jira queries. <strong>It separates fact from inference:</strong> <em>"Facts above are tool-derived; the … points are my inferences/recommendations."</em></p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">I re-ran that question for this edition. It timed out <strong>again — 3 of 3</strong>. This time it degraded better: fell back to the Roadmap page KPIs (same live Jira data, already in Cosmos), gave a quantified answer on throughput and WIP, and refused only what it couldn't source.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">So I read the code. <strong>Root cause:</strong> <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">TOOL_TIMEOUT_MS = 5000</code> is the default. Production tools were recognised as slow and given a 30s override. <strong>No Jira tool was ever added to that map</strong> — so a cross-internet Atlassian call gets the same five seconds as an in-region Cosmos point read. Full write-up.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The proposed fix isn't a bigger number: derive the timeout from a declared <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">executionClass</code> and add a contract test asserting every outbound non-Azure tool is declared <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">external-api</code>. <strong>That turns a silent default into a build failure.</strong></p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">The pattern worth naming: beware the opt-in registry</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Two of this platform's real bugs are the same shape — the missed model tier and the Jira timeout. A map that new items must be added to, where forgetting fails <strong>silently</strong>. Derive from a declared property; test the derivation.</p>

<h2 style="font-size:1.5em;line-height:1.25;margin:2.6em 0 .2em;color:#c2410c;letter-spacing:-.01em;border-top:3px solid #c2410c;padding-top:.7em">Part V — The agent platform</h2>

<h2 style="font-size:1.5em;line-height:1.25;margin:2.6em 0 .2em;color:#c2410c;letter-spacing:-.01em;border-top:3px solid #c2410c;padding-top:.7em">Part VI — Governance</h2>

<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">19. The competency map — and why it isn't all fives</h2>

<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Why the low ones are low, stated plainly:</strong></p>
<ul style="margin:1.1em 0 1.1em 1.3em;padding:0;line-height:1.65"><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Evals — 2.</strong> World-class <em>contract</em> tests; until recently zero <em>quality</em> evals. <p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">A matrix with no low scores is a marketing document. These four are exactly what I would want to be hired to close.</p>
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">20. Lessons</h2>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Build the calculation engine first</strong>, as a pure function.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Give agents tools, not documents.</strong></p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Enforce policy where the data is, not where the prompt is.</strong></p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>You cannot make a model behave by instructing it harder — change what is possible.</strong></p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Keep the deterministic layer deterministic.</strong></p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Sequence the boring dependency first.</strong> Identity before Movement Pack; Movement Pack before agents.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Prove conservation before you publish.</strong></p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>A silent no-op is worse than a crash.</strong> The scheduler's <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">findDue</code> filtered on <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">c.enabled = true</code> — a field <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">AgentDefinitionV1</code> does not have. The predicate never matched, so <strong>no scheduled agent run could ever fire</strong>, and nothing errored.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Beware the opt-in registry.</strong></p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Write contracts, not prompts.</strong> Or: <strong>don't just vibe — build.</strong></p>
<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">21. The honest scorecard and the backlog</h2>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">A June 2026 maturity audit against ~60 primary sources, produced by a multi-agent workflow that <strong>adversarially verified every claimed gap — disproving 13 of 26.</strong> Verdict: <strong>Level 3 of 5</strong> — 4s on grounding, tooling and clarification; <strong>2s on evals, observability, memory and durable execution</strong>. Sharpest finding: a recurring <strong>"documented-but-not-enforced"</strong> risk.</p>

<h2 style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">22. FAQ — the questions I actually get asked</h2>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">These are the objections a CTO raises in the first ten minutes. None of them have a defensive answer, so here are the real ones.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"Why didn't you just use LangChain?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The honest answer first: <strong>because the point was to learn this from the ground up.</strong> I wanted to understand what a tool loop, a context window, a routing decision and a hallucination <em>actually are</em> — not to learn a framework's opinion about them. That was the primary goal, and a framework would have defeated it.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">But there's an architectural answer too, and it holds up independently. Here is the suite against what this platform needed:</p>
<div style="overflow-x:auto;margin:1.4em 0"><table style="border-collapse:collapse;width:100%;font-size:.94em;min-width:520px"><thead><tr><th style="text-align:left;padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:2px solid #e3e8ee;color:#c2410c;font-size:.9em;white-space:nowrap">LangChain product</th><th style="text-align:left;padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:2px solid #e3e8ee;color:#c2410c;font-size:.9em;white-space:nowrap">What it gives you</th><th style="text-align:left;padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:2px solid #e3e8ee;color:#c2410c;font-size:.9em;white-space:nowrap">What I did instead</th><th style="text-align:left;padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:2px solid #e3e8ee;color:#c2410c;font-size:.9em;white-space:nowrap">Verdict</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top"><strong>LangChain (OSS framework)</strong></td><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top">Model/tool/prompt abstractions, chains, integrations across providers</td><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top">Direct Anthropic Messages API + a frozen <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">AiProviderTransport</code> contract</td><td style="padding:.5em .6em;border-bottom:1px solid #eef1f5;vertical-align:top"><strong>Not needed.</strong> Its value is portability across many providers. <p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The load-bearing point: <strong>the agent loop was never the hard part.</strong> Anthropic's Agent SDK handed me the tool loop, sub-agents, hooks, permissions and sessions — the research note that locked this in says the SDK "saves us from rebuilding the <em>runtime / orchestration</em> layer," turning v0 into "days of work, not weeks."</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The hard part was everything a framework can't do for you: ABAC enforced <em>inside</em> tool handlers before data reaches the model; the Movement Pack that pre-computes typed, ranked, content-addressed deltas so the model <strong>narrates rather than calculates</strong>; a 170-entry governed insight catalog; SAP reconciliation that refuses to publish on row-count drift. LangChain has no opinion about any of that, because those are <em>my</em> business's invariants. Adopting a framework would have added an abstraction layer over the easy 20% while leaving the hard 80% exactly where it was.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Then the frugality constraint, which is real and not a rhetorical flourish. This runs on a shoestring for ~12 internal users. LangSmith's Plus tier is <strong>$39/seat/month plus trace overages</strong>, and a typical production LangSmith + LangGraph Platform setup lands in the <strong>$175–375/month</strong> range before model spend. For a one-person team that is a material line item, and every dependency is also a thing that can break, change its licence, or reprice.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72"><strong>Where I'd change my mind — and it's the same place both times.</strong> My two weakest dimensions are <strong>evals (2/5)</strong> and <strong>observability (2/5)</strong>. Those are precisely LangSmith's core competence. The sane move isn't a framework rewrite; it's <strong>adopting LangSmith (or promptfoo/Braintrust/DeepEval) for the eval and trace layer specifically</strong>, against a codebase whose interfaces are already frozen and contract-tested. I'd be buying the gap, not the religion.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"Why go all-in on Claude? Why not build a provider-agnostic router?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">I considered it, and the architecture still carries the seam. <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">AiProviderTransport</code> is a <strong>frozen, asserted contract</strong> — the orchestration layer talks to <em>a provider</em>, not to Anthropic. <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">providerCapabilities.js</code> says so in its own header: adding OpenAI or Gemini "would add sibling files (<code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">openai.js</code>, <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">gemini.js</code>) with the same <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">classify()</code> / <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">resolveThinkingConfig()</code> contract."</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">So let me be precise, because this is where people overclaim: <strong>there is a seam, not a router.</strong> One implementation exists. The maturity audit scores this dimension <strong>3/5</strong> and names the gap exactly — "sophisticated single-provider routing; no cross-provider failover."</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Why I stopped there is a resourcing decision, not an architectural belief. <strong>When you're building fast, you pick a platform and move.</strong> I'm one person running an entire business; a provider-abstraction layer is a permanent tax on every feature, paid daily, against a risk that has not yet materialised. So I went <strong>vertically integrated</strong> on Anthropic — not just the LLM API but the Agent SDK, the MCP tool surface, the hook model. That verticality is what made the agent framework a days-long effort instead of a months-long one.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">What I do have inside that bet: model <strong>auto-discovery</strong> every 12 hours against <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">/v1/models</code>, tier classification, and a <strong>health circuit breaker that demotes and recovers automatically</strong>. So there <em>is</em> automatic failover — within the family, not across vendors. If Anthropic has a total outage, the AI features degrade; the rest of the platform doesn't, because every number on every screen comes from Cosmos and the model only narrates.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">I'd revisit on a hard trigger: a sustained availability problem, a pricing shift, or a capability that only exists elsewhere. The seam is there so that day is an implementation, not a rewrite.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"Why Cosmos DB and not a relational database?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">It began as a cost decision and stayed for engineering reasons.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">It started as a proof of concept on a shoestring, and the thing quietly grew into an enterprise application. But a document store structured with discipline turned out to fit this problem better than the relational default:</p>
<ul style="margin:1.1em 0 1.1em 1.3em;padding:0;line-height:1.65"><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Schema evolution without migrations.</strong> Fiscal-year structures, contract shapes and connector outputs changed repeatedly over eight months. Not one of those changes needed a migration window.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>The ETL has no impedance mismatch.</strong> SAP exports arrive as records and land as documents. A connector run produces artefacts and syncs documents — no ORM, no staging schema, no object-relational mapping between the export and the store. That is most of why the ETL is ten connectors and not a data-engineering department.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>The partition key <em>is</em> the security boundary.</strong> Everything partitions on <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">/pk</code>, carrying business unit and fiscal year. The authorization rule is <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">hasPermission AND hasBuScope AND ownsResource</code> — BU scope is enforced physically in the storage layout, not just in a <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">WHERE</code> clause someone can forget.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Operational features I'd otherwise have built.</strong> TTL gives one-year audit retention as a field, not a cron job. ETags give optimistic concurrency without a locking strategy.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>One dependency, one region, an emulator for local dev.</strong> <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">npm run dev:up</code> boots the emulator; there is no second database technology to run, secure, back up or patch.</li></ul>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The honest counterpoint: <strong>cross-container joins and aggregations are my problem, not the engine's.</strong> They're hand-written and app-side. A relational database would make ad-hoc analytical queries dramatically easier — and that is <em>exactly</em> why the AI reads a <strong>governed semantic layer of ~170 curated insights instead of writing SQL.</strong> The database choice and the "tools, not text-to-SQL" choice are the same decision seen from two angles.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"Why a fixed tool catalog instead of text-to-SQL?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Because a generated query cannot be authorized. Every one of the 83 tools enforces ABAC <em>inside the handler</em>, before a single row reaches the model, and returns citations the hooks accumulate. Text-to-SQL inverts that: the model composes the access path and you audit afterwards. It also can't be governed — dbt's own 2026 benchmark makes the case for a semantic layer over text-to-SQL, and the insight catalog is that layer, versioned with formulas, assumptions and sources.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"How do you stop it inventing numbers?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Structurally, not by asking it nicely. Deterministic tool handlers emit the citations and chart datasets; <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">PostToolUse</code> hooks accumulate them; the model is <strong>not the source of provenance</strong>. The Movement Pack pre-computes typed, ranked, ABAC-scoped deltas with content-addressed IDs so the model narrates a computed fact rather than performing arithmetic. And the clarification gate <strong>withholds the data</strong> below a confidence threshold instead of instructing the model to be careful.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">The honest part: the <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">ClaimV1</code>/<code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">FactV1</code> refine gate currently passes trivially, because a boilerplate fact is always attached. The audit caught it, it's documented, and it's on the near-term list. A guarantee that always passes is not a guarantee.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"What's the weakest part of the platform?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Durable execution. The dispatch queue is written but has no consumer, so a process restart orphans in-flight runs; the scheduler's stale-lease window (5 min) is shorter than the run timeout (15 min), which is a latent double-fire. It's a documented Phase 4 deferral rather than an oversight, and the client mitigates with auto-resume — but it is the single biggest reliability gap, and it's the one place LangGraph's checkpointing would genuinely have bought me something.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"No evals? In a financial application?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Correct, and it's the top of the backlog. What exists is the opposite trade: <strong>~1,100+ deterministic contract tests</strong>, frozen versioned schemas, and a named regression test for every incident (penalty-netting, orderbook double-count, M20-cabinet). Every model call in every test is a deterministic fake. That gives me strong guarantees about <em>behaviour</em> and none about <em>answer quality</em>. The probabilistic layer goes on top of the deterministic gate — not instead of it.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"How does one person keep this from rotting?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">A written contract with a pre-review self-check that exists because of specific, embarrassing misses: assert the observable result rather than the intermediate you produced; diff <strong>both</strong> sides of every interface; run the full gate after touching a contract-tested artefact; enumerate every entry point; no silent caps; no prompt-only correctness. The recurring root cause it was written to kill: <em>stopping at "my code does what I intended" instead of "the consumer gets the right result."</em> Appendix A is that same lesson failing eight more times in a single afternoon.</p>
<h3 style="font-size:1.13em;line-height:1.35;margin:1.7em 0 .45em;color:#1f2933">"What would you do differently?"</h3>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Instrument with OpenTelemetry from day one — retrofitting <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">gen_ai.*</code> spans over a bespoke correlation scheme is pure rework. Build the eval harness before feature number twenty, not after. And enforce before declaring: the sharpest finding in the audit was a class of controls that were <em>persisted yet inert</em> — an admin sets <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">restrictedFields</code> in the UI and believes it works. Documentation that describes runtime behaviour you haven't written is worse than no documentation, for the same reason a test that agrees with your bug is worse than no test.</p>
<hr style="border:0;border-top:1px solid #e3e8ee;margin:2.4em 0">
<h2 id="appendix-a" style="font-size:1.42em;line-height:1.3;margin:2.1em 0 .55em;color:#1f2933;letter-spacing:-.01em">Appendix A — how these screenshots were made</h2>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Every image is the live production system with real data. A redaction layer is injected before capture; capture <strong>loops</strong> — redact, verify, repeat — and writes a PNG only once the page verifies clean.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">It failed <strong>eight</strong> times, and every failure is the same lesson wearing a different hat: <em>my checker only saw what my redactor saw.</em></p>
<ol style="margin:1.1em 0 1.1em 1.3em;padding:0;line-height:1.65"><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Both scanned leaf elements.</strong> Prose inside <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">&lt;li&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Facts:&lt;/strong&gt;…&lt;/li&gt;</code> is not a leaf; real amounts reached a screenshot while the check said "clean".</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Pattern matching was the wrong model.</strong> An answer named five customers in prose with no currency, no suffix, no digits. <strong>You cannot enumerate what sensitive text looks like</strong> — so AI output became sensitive <em>by default</em>.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>I reintroduced bug 1 in the fix for bug 2.</strong></li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Form values are rendered text that is not a text node.</strong> <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">&lt;input value="Elihle"&gt;</code> shows an employee's name; a <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">TreeWalker</code> never sees it. The Capacity Planner roster published four real first names.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Names that only exist in the page's own data.</strong> The Sales Command Center renders the active account manager as a scope chip. No hardcoded regex can know that "ZIYAAD" is a person — but the page's own Account Manager filter can. The fix <strong>harvests names from the people-picker options and redacts them everywhere</strong>, which generalises to any business unit without editing the code.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Tenant identifiers that look like ordinary codes.</strong> A business-unit slug and a Jira project key are indistinguishable from a product code to a regex. Hardcoding them in the redactor would have repeated this repo's own <strong>opt-in-registry</strong> bug — the pattern behind both the missed model tier and the Jira timeout, where forgetting an entry fails <em>silently</em>. They live in one JSON file that feeds the redactor <strong>and</strong> the checker, so adding a term extends both at once.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>The length threshold was the wrong test for tables.</strong> AI output is blurred by default above 25 characters — but the model also answers with <strong>markdown tables</strong>, and a Jira issue title is 17 characters. Cells slipped under the bar and published. Length never described the risk; <em>being tabular data</em> did. Cells are now blurred unconditionally inside an AI surface.</li><li style="margin:.4em 0"><strong>Verified clean, captured dirty.</strong> The worst one. The loop proved the DOM was clean <em>before</em> the screenshot — the intermediate, not the deliverable. Taking a clipped capture resizes the rendering surface, Recharts re-renders its axes, and brand-new <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">&lt;tspan&gt;</code>s carrying real currency (<code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">R 400 000</code>, <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">R 1 600 000</code>) were painted into the PNG <strong>after</strong> the check passed. The run logged <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">$=0</code> and the file leaked. The assertion now <strong>straddles</strong> the capture: shoot, re-verify, re-shoot if the page moved — so the number reported describes the file on disk.</li></ol>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">Mechanical gotchas: <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">captureBeyondViewport</code> renders offscreen content <em>at capture time</em>, i.e. after redaction; CSS <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">filter</code> does nothing on an SVG <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">&lt;tspan&gt;</code>; <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">element.click()</code> does not close a React dropdown, because outside-click handlers listen for <code style="background:#f4f6f8;border:1px solid #e3e8ee;border-radius:3px;padding:.08em .32em;font-size:.9em;font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,Consolas,monospace">mousedown</code> — which left the fiscal-year panel open across <em>every</em> screenshot of one entire run; and the physical mouse cursor sits wherever it was left, so a chart re-rendering underneath it draws a hover tooltip straight into the capture.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">There is a pleasing symmetry with the SEV1 in §20 and the Jira bug. <strong>A test that agrees with your bug is worse than no test</strong> — it converts an unknown risk into false confidence. The fix, every time, was to assert the <em>observable outcome</em> rather than re-implement the producer's logic.</p>
<p style="margin:1.05em 0;line-height:1.72">One screenshot is deliberately published readable — the "it refuses" answer in §18. Every visible string was reviewed by hand.</p>
